Water Damage Restoration in Downtown North Chicago

North Chicago’s station-area planning study places the downtown core along Sheridan Road, from Foss Park Avenue on the north to 18th Street on the south. The study describes retail along that stretch, a Metra Union Pacific North Line station and housing nearby.

That mix makes downtown different from a street of single-family houses. A leak in one unit or storefront can reach the space below or beside it, and the people affected may include a tenant, a landlord and a shop owner. This page explains how we handle that kind of loss.

What Kinds of Buildings Are Here

The 2008 station-area plan lists single-family houses, two-flats, townhouses, attached housing of 2 to 8 units, apartment and condominium buildings, and mixed-use buildings with apartments above stores. It describes the downtown’s mixed-use buildings as historic and recommends preserving and renovating them where appropriate.

City-wide figures from the Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning show the same pattern. About 67 percent of occupied homes in North Chicago are rented, and roughly 38 percent of housing units are in buildings of 2 to 19 units. Those are city numbers, not downtown counts.

How We Handle Multi-Unit and Mixed-Use Losses

We start by finding where the water began and which spaces it reached. A technician measures moisture in walls, floors and ceilings with meters, because water often travels farther than the visible stain.

Then the crew extracts standing water and removes materials that cannot be dried. They set air movers and dehumidifiers and log readings until the structure is dry. The process follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration.

For businesses, our commercial water damage page explains scope, records and working around opening hours. For the drying stage, see structural drying and dehumidification.

Street Work on Broadway Avenue

The city announced storm sewer improvements on Broadway Avenue beginning July 20, 2026. The work includes storm sewer installation, curb and gutter, sidewalks, driveway replacement and paving, and the city warned that access to streets and parking within the construction zone may be interrupted.

If you call from a street with active work, tell us. Equipment placement and parking can change how a crew sets up, and the city’s notice did not report any flooding as the reason for the project.

Landlords, Tenants and the Paper Trail

In a rental, the lease and the building owner’s policy decide who reports a loss, so tenants should tell the landlord quickly. Photograph the damage, keep receipts for emergency costs and write down when you first noticed the water.

We provide photos, moisture readings and a written scope that each insurer can review. Whether a loss is covered is decided by the policy and the insurer, and the Illinois Department of Insurance advises reporting damage promptly.

Safety Steps Before a Crew Arrives

Do these only if it is safe

  • ✓Move out of rooms with ceilings that sag or drip, because wet plaster and drywall can fall.
  • ✓Close the shutoff for the failed fixture if you can reach it, or ask the building manager to do it.
  • ✓Stay away from standing water near outlets, cords and appliances.
  • ✓Photograph the damage in every affected unit before anything is moved.

Sewage or drain backups are contaminated water and need protective gear, so call us instead of cleaning them up yourself. If you smell gas or see an electrical hazard, leave the building and call emergency services first.

If Water Is Still Spreading

Water in a shared building spreads quickly, so these steps come first.

Find the source

Close the shutoff for the failed fixture, or ask the building manager or landlord to close the valve for the unit or the riser.

Clear the room below

If a ceiling is stained or sagging, move people and valuables out from under it. Wet plaster can drop without warning.

Record every unit

Photograph each affected apartment or storefront, including the ceiling, walls and floors. Write down who reported what and when.

Call and describe

Call the 24/7 line and tell us the address, which floors are wet and whether the water is clean, gray or from a drain.
