How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in North Chicago

Wet photos and papers can often be saved if you act quickly and gently. This guide covers what to do in the first two days, and where the limits are.

Published by North Chicago Water Damage Pros · Updated November 2026
24/7 Emergency Dispatch(847) 368-7353☎ Call Now

The First 48 Hours Matter Most

Paper, photos and books start to grow mold in about two days in damp conditions, and they get harder to separate as they dry stuck together. Move quickly, but do not rush the handling, because wet paper tears easily.

If the water was clean, such as a leak from a supply line, most paper items have a good chance of being saved. If it was sewage or flood water, only items of real value should be dried, and a specialist is a better choice than doing it at home.

Safety Before Salvage

Before you reach into water or damp boxes

  • ✓Make sure the area is safe to enter and the power is off if water is near outlets.
  • ✓Wear gloves, and wear a mask if you see mold or the water was contaminated.
  • ✓Do not enter standing water. Wait for the crew to remove it, then recover items.
  • ✓Photograph the items where you found them, since that helps with insurance.

What to Do for Each Kind of Item

ItemBest first stepNotes
Loose documents and papersAir dry flat, or freeze if you cannot dry them within a day or twoDo not pull wet sheets apart if they are stuck. Freezing holds them until a specialist can help
PhotographsRinse gently in clean water if mud-coated, then air dry face up on clean paperDo not touch the image surface. Photos that stick together can be separated while wet
BooksStand them up, fan the pages slightly and air dry, or freeze if too many to dryHeavily soaked books usually need a specialist
Certificates and legal papersFreeze and consult a conservator for originalsReplacement copies are often available from the issuing office
Negatives and slidesKeep wet and rinse in clean water, then air dryDo not let them dry while stuck together
Digital media and drivesDo not power on. See our guidance on electronicsData recovery is a specialist service

Freezing is a common conservator tactic for buying time. A freezer stops mold and keeps wet paper stable until a professional can freeze-dry it. This is general archival practice and not a guarantee for every item.

How to Air Dry Papers and Photos

1

Set up a clean, dry space

Use a room with low humidity and good airflow, such as a spare room with a dehumidifier running.

2

Lay items flat

Place photos face up on clean paper towels or unprinted newsprint. Keep items from touching each other.

3

Keep air moving

A fan on low across the room, not at the items, speeds drying without blowing paper around.

4

Change the blotting paper

Replace damp paper towels as they absorb moisture.

5

Flatten under weight once nearly dry

Place a clean board and light weights on top, and wait until completely dry before storing.

Do not use a hair dryer, oven or microwave to speed things up. Heat warps paper and can set stains, and sunlight fades ink and color.

What a Specialist Can Do That You Cannot

Document restoration specialists use vacuum freeze drying and conservation methods to save large volumes of wet records, books and artwork. For irreplaceable family albums, legal records and business files, a specialist is often worth it.

We handle the building, not the paper, but we can tell you which areas are safe to enter and when. Our structural drying process also brings down room humidity, which protects papers that survived the first hit.

Insurance Notes on Contents

Personal property is typically covered under a different part of a homeowners policy than the structure, and it may have separate limits. Photograph damaged items before discarding them, and ask your insurer how they want valuable papers and photographs documented.

Preventing the Next Loss for Your Records

Once things are dry, store papers and photos where water is less likely to reach. Use sealed plastic bins on shelves rather than cardboard boxes on the floor, and keep important originals on an upper level.

ItemBetter storage
Family photosArchival boxes or albums on an upper shelf, plus digital copies
Legal papersA sealed bin on an upper floor, with scans stored elsewhere
Tax and financial recordsDigital copies and a labeled bin off the floor
BooksShelves away from exterior walls and above floor level

Scanning your most important papers and photos is the cheapest protection. A copy stored in cloud storage or on a drive kept elsewhere survives a flood that destroys the original.

Working With Family and Archivists

Family photographs often matter to several people. Gather relatives who can help identify and sort prints while they dry, and ask a local historical society or library whether it can point you to a conservator for rare items. A short phone call is often enough to get started.

Where the Handling Advice Comes From

The room-level drying that protects papers follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. Paper conservation itself is a separate specialty, so treat the steps above as general first aid and ask a conservator about irreplaceable items.

Protecting Records in Lake County Homes

Basements and attics are where families keep boxes of photos and papers, and they are also where leaks and flooding show up first. Lake County has had repeated flood events, and the April 2013 rainfall caused urban flooding across northeastern Illinois.

Moving boxes onto shelves and into sealed plastic bins is a simple, cheap step. We serve North Chicago and also respond in Winthrop Harbor and Fort Sheridan, and we tell you what to move first when you call.
