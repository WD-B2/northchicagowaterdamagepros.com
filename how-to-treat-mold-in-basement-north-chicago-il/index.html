How to Treat Mold in a North Chicago Basement: Control the Moisture

Mold treatment is mostly moisture control. This guide walks through finding where the water comes from, lowering humidity and keeping the basement dry, which is what stops mold from coming back.

Published by North Chicago Water Damage Pros · Updated November 2026
24/7 Emergency Dispatch(847) 368-7353☎ Call Now

Why Treating the Surface Is Not Enough

Mold needs three things: a surface to grow on, a food source such as dust or drywall paper and moisture. The first two are in every basement, so the only one you can control is moisture.

That is why wiping a wall and walking away rarely works. If the humidity stays high or water keeps seeping in, growth returns, sometimes within weeks. Treatment means finding and shutting off the moisture supply first.

Where Basement Moisture Comes From

SourceHow to recognize itTypical fix
Surface water and poor gradingDamp walls after rain, water near the foundationRegrade soil away from the house, extend downspouts
Gutters and downspoutsOverflow at corners, damp spots below the gutter lineClean and repair gutters, discharge water away from the wall
Window wellsWater around basement windowsClear debris, add covers, repair drainage
Plumbing leaksStain below a pipe, rising water billPlumber repairs, then drying. See burst pipe water damage
Sump pump failureWater in the pit, standing water after rainTest and replace the pump, add a backup
Condensation on cold surfacesWet cold pipes, foggy windows, damp airInsulate pipes, run a dehumidifier
Laundry and dryer humidityMoist air near the laundry areaVent the dryer outdoors, add a vent fan

Most of these fixes are done by other trades, such as a plumber, a gutter installer, a landscaper or a waterproofing contractor. We clean and dry the damage and tell you what the readings show.

Lowering Basement Humidity

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent and ideally between 30 and 50 percent. A basement is often well above that in summer, when warm, humid air meets cool walls and floors.

A hygrometer is an inexpensive meter that shows relative humidity. If readings stay high, a dehumidifier sized for the space helps, and it needs a place to drain, either a hose to a floor drain or a regular emptying of the tank.

Practical humidity steps

  • ✓Place a hygrometer in the basement and check it weekly through summer.
  • ✓Run a dehumidifier when readings climb, and empty or drain it consistently.
  • ✓Keep stored items off the floor and away from exterior walls.
  • ✓Vent clothes dryers and bathroom fans outdoors, not into the basement.
  • ✓Avoid drying laundry on racks in the basement.

Treating Existing Growth

Small spots on hard surfaces can be cleaned with detergent, protective gear and thorough drying, as covered in our related guidance on basement cleaning. Porous materials such as drywall, carpet and insulation with growth are usually removed.

The Illinois Department of Public Health advises that a professional clean mold areas over about 10 square feet and that wet areas be dried within 24 to 48 hours. For larger growth, containment and HEPA filtration keep spores from spreading, which is the work described on our mold remediation page.

Drying What Is Still Wet

If water has entered recently, drying comes first. Extract standing water, lift wet carpet and pad and run dehumidifiers and air movers until moisture readings return to normal. Our water extraction and structural drying pages explain what a crew does.

Damp walls, no standing waterFind the source and dehumidifyClean surface growth if present

Waterproofing and What Cleanup Cannot Do

We do not install waterproofing systems, and a cleanup company should not promise that water will never return. If water comes through the wall or the floor after every storm, the fix is drainage work such as exterior grading, drain tile or a sump system.

A cleanup crew can document where the moisture is highest, which helps a waterproofing contractor design the right fix. That honest division of work saves homeowners from paying twice for the same problem.

A Seasonal Moisture Checklist

Basement moisture follows the calendar. Spring brings thaw and heavy rain, summer brings humid air, fall brings leaves that clog gutters and winter brings frozen lines. A short checklist once a season catches problems early.

SeasonWhat to check
SpringSump pump test, window wells, damp spots after the first heavy rain
SummerHumidity readings, dehumidifier drain, condensation on cold pipes
FallGutters and downspouts, grading near the foundation, window well covers
WinterExposed pipes, garage and utility room temperatures, basement windows

Signs Your Plan Is Working

A working moisture plan shows up in plain signs. Humidity readings stay in a steady range, walls stay dry after storms, the musty smell fades and no new spots appear after a month. If any of those slip, return to the source list and check each item again.

Standards for Larger Mold Jobs

When growth is large enough to need a crew, the reference is ANSI/IICRC S520, the standard for professional mold remediation, which covers containment, filtration and verifying that the moisture source is controlled. Small spots can be handled with careful cleaning, as described above.

Safety Steps Before Treating Basement Mold

Do these first

  • ✓Wear a fitted respirator, gloves and eye protection for any hands-on work.
  • ✓Keep children, pets and anyone sensitive out of the area.
  • ✓Do not run the furnace fan while you disturb growth, since ducts can spread spores.

Basement Humidity Along Lake Michigan

July in Lake County averages near 82 degrees Fahrenheit and the area gets about 36 inches of precipitation a year. Warm, humid summer air condenses on cool basement surfaces, and spring thaw loads the soil with water.

We serve North Chicago and also respond in Winthrop Harbor and Fort Sheridan, where basement moisture shows up on the same seasonal pattern.
