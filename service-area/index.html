About the Shoreline Where We Work

Lake County sits on Lake Michigan and averages about 36 inches of precipitation a year. The lake moderates some weather and adds wind, humidity and storms, which explains why basement water, frozen pipes and mold show up so often along this shore.

Drying and cleanup in every area follow the reasoning in ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration, so the plan is the same whether you are in North Chicago or a nearby shore town.

If the problem is on the utility side of the meter, such as a main break, call the water provider for your address. If water is inside the building, call us, and we work on the cleanup while the provider works on the supply.

How Dispatch Works for Nearby Addresses

When you call from a nearby town, we ask for your street and the type of water problem. We tell you right away whether a crew can reach you, and what to do while you wait, such as shutting off the water and lifting items off the floor.

We do not promise a set arrival time, because traffic, weather and call volume change it. We do promise a straight answer about coverage and about what the first step should be.

What We Ask When You Call From Any Area

Details that help dispatch

  • ✓The street address and the nearest cross street.
  • ✓What kind of water it is, such as clean supply water, rain or sewage.
  • ✓Which floors and rooms are wet, and roughly how deep.
  • ✓Whether the source has been shut off.
  • ✓Whether anyone is in the building and whether power is on in the wet area.

Those answers let us tell you what to do right away and which crew and equipment to send. They also help us tell you honestly whether the job is something we can reach.
