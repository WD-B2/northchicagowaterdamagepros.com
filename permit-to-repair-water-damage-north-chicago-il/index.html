Permit to Repair Water Damage in North Chicago, IL

Cleanup and drying usually do not need a permit, but rebuild work sometimes does. This guide shows what the North Chicago Building Department publishes and how to check before you start repairs.

Published by North Chicago Water Damage Pros · Updated November 2026
24/7 Emergency Dispatch(847) 368-7353☎ Call Now

Cleanup Versus Repair: Where Permits Come In

Water damage jobs have stages. Emergency cleanup and drying protect the building, and repair rebuilds what was removed. Permits are usually a repair question, and whether one is needed depends on the work.

We handle extraction, drying and cleanup and do not pull permits for rebuild. If your repairs involve structure, electrical, plumbing or other regulated work, your contractor or the property owner should ask the city first. When in doubt, ask.

Which Codes Apply

North Chicago has adopted the 2021 International Building Code, Residential Code and Property Maintenance Code, effective October 1, 2024. Those codes shape structural repairs, framing, electrical, plumbing and mechanical work, along with rental property conditions.

The city also posts a plumber registration application and a contractor registration packet. If you hire out repair work, ask your contractor whether they are registered with the city and who pulls the permit.

Scheduling Inspections

The city asks that inspections be scheduled well in advance. If a permit is required, build the inspection into your repair timeline and keep the area open until the inspector has seen it when that is required.

Our moisture logs and photographs can help show that framing and subfloor were dry before closing walls. See our wall and drywall repair page for how readings feed the repair stage.

Rental Properties

North Chicago requires an annual landlord license for rental properties. Landlords should keep records of water damage and repairs, since tenants, insurers and city inspectors may all ask what was done.

Tell your property manager or landlord early after a leak, and keep written records of dates, photos and repair invoices.

Insurance and Permits

Your insurer may ask about permits and licensed contractors for repair work, and some repairs depend on documentation. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises waiting on large permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected the damage.

Keep copies of permits, inspection results and invoices. They are part of the claim record, and they help if you sell the property later.

A Practical Order of Operations

Permits slow projects only when they are handled late. Handling them in the right order keeps the timeline smooth and avoids rework.

1

Stop and dry

Stop the source and dry the building. Cleanup and drying usually come before any permit questions.

2

Get the readings

Ask for the moisture log and photographs from the cleanup crew so repair planning starts with facts.

3

Ask the city

Call the Building Department with a plain description of the repair work and ask what applies.

4

Hire registered professionals

Ask contractors and plumbers whether they hold the city registrations the department posts, and who pulls each permit.

5

Schedule inspections

Book inspections early, since the city asks for scheduling well in advance, and keep walls open until required inspections are done.

What to Bring When You Call

Have your address, a short description of the damage and the repairs you are planning. If you have a contractor, bring the name and the scope of work. The more specific you are, the more useful the answer.

Write down the name of the person you speak to, the date and what was said. A short note can settle a later disagreement about what the city told you, and it belongs in the same folder as your photos and invoices.

Keeping Records for the Sale or Claim

Permits, inspection results and invoices matter later. Keep them with your photos and moisture readings in one folder, and ask contractors for final paperwork before you pay the last invoice. That folder answers questions from insurers, lenders and buyers without a scramble.

Standards and Codes Together

Building codes govern repair, while ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference for the drying that comes before it. The moisture log from the S500-style process is the record that shows framing was dry before walls were closed.

Permit Questions Near Lake Michigan

Basement waterproofing, sump work and drainage changes can involve grading and plumbing, so ask the city about them. Lake County's wet springs and the April 2013 flooding show why these projects are common.

We help North Chicago residents and also respond in Winthrop Harbor and Fort Sheridan, where permits come from the village or city that covers your address. Fort Sheridan addresses fall in Lake Forest, Highwood or Highland Park, so confirm which applies. For basement work, see basement water damage cleanup.
