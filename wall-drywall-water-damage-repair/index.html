Wet drywall looks fine until it crumbles or sprouts mold. We map the moisture, remove only what has to go and dry what can stay.

What Water Does to a Wall

Drywall is gypsum between paper, and paper holds water and food for mold. A stain or bubble usually means water has been in the wall longer than it looks, and the visible mark is just where it reached the surface.

What you seeWhat it usually meansFirst move
Brown stain with a ringWater reached the surface and dried, perhaps more than onceFind the source and check for ongoing moisture
Bubbling or peeling paintMoisture behind the paint layerDo not paint over it. Measure moisture first
Soft or crumbling drywallProlonged saturationCut out and replace
Musty smell in the roomHidden dampness or moldInspect the wall cavity
Baseboard gap or swellingWater at the base of the wallCheck for wicking above the floor

The paint-bubble story is common enough to deserve its own detail. A bubble means the moisture is trapped behind a sealed layer, so scraping and repainting without drying only starts the cycle again.

How We Decide What to Cut and What to Dry

Technicians use pin and pinless moisture meters and a thermal camera to outline the wet area. If drywall is saturated, crumbling or moldy, it is removed. If it is only damp and the water was clean, it may be dried in place.

For flood and sewage losses, a flood cut removes the lower portion of the wall so the framing behind can dry. After removal, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavity and studs, the same way described in our structural drying section.

Repair Prep After Drying

The crew stops at a dry, clean opening. Drywall hanging, taping, texture and paint are done by a drywall or painting contractor. We can give you moisture readings and a written scope so the contractor knows the wall is ready.

When Mold Is in the Wall

Dark spots, fuzzy growth and a persistent smell point to mold. The Illinois Department of Public Health advises that a professional should clean mold areas larger than about 10 square feet, and that drying wet areas within 24 to 48 hours limits growth.

If the area is large, our mold remediation page explains containment and removal.

Insurance and Wall Damage

Sudden leaks that damage walls are commonly covered, and long-term seepage often is not. Photograph the wall before opening it and again after, since both views help the adjuster.

Standard Behind Wall Drying Decisions

Choices about cutting or drying a wall follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard reference for water damage restoration, which links decisions to water category and measured moisture. That is how we explain why one wall is dried in place and another is opened.

Safety Steps Before Opening a Wall

Do these first

  • ✓Turn off power to outlets on a wet wall at the breaker before cutting.
  • ✓Check for plumbing and wiring behind the area before you cut.
  • ✓Wear a mask and gloves, since wet drywall can hold mold and dust.

Walls in North Chicago Buildings

North Chicago adopted the 2021 International Building Code and Residential Code as of October 1, 2024, and repair work that involves structure or permits goes through the Building Department at 1850 Lewis Avenue. Cosmetic drywall repair often needs no permit, but check with the department when in doubt.

We serve North Chicago and also Winthrop Harbor and Fort Sheridan. If the stain is on a ceiling instead, see ceiling water damage.
