Emergency Burst Pipe Response: First Steps Before the Crew Arrives

Dispatch for a burst pipe is available 24/7, and the single most useful thing you can do while waiting is stop the flow. Find your main shutoff, usually where the water line enters the house near the meter, and turn it fully clockwise.

In the first few minutes

  • ✓Shut off the main water valve, then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain what remains in the lines.
  • ✓Switch off electricity to wet areas at the panel only if you can reach it dry. Otherwise stay away and wait for the crew.
  • ✓Move valuables, paperwork and electronics above the waterline and photograph the damage.
  • ✓Do not use ceiling fixtures under a wet ceiling. Water pooled above a light can fall without warning.

If the pipe you suspect is on the city side of the meter, the North Chicago Water Department keeps an after-hours emergency line at 847-596-8774. Anything inside your walls and past the meter belongs to the property owner and a plumber.

Where Burst Pipes Release the Most Water

The location of the break decides how much damage you get. A split in an exposed basement line mostly wets the floor. A failure inside a wall or ceiling soaks insulation and drywall before anyone notices.

Supply lines under pressure

Household supply runs at constant pressure, so a failed fitting keeps pushing water until the valve is closed.

Upper-floor breaks

Water runs down through floor cavities and shows up as ceiling stains or drips in the room below.

Hidden wall cavities

A pinhole or split joint can feed a wall for days. Moisture meters and thermal imaging find the extent.

Aging or corroded pipe

Old galvanized and some failing plastic fittings can let go at a joint with little warning.

Winter breaks are a separate case, and the frozen pipe water damage page explains how thaw timing changes the response.

Do Not Wait for the Ceiling to Fall

If water is coming through a ceiling or wall, call now and keep people out of that room.

What the Cleanup Involves After the Leak Stops

Once the water is off, the crew starts with extraction to clear standing water. Technicians then map moisture in walls, floors and ceilings with meters so they know what is wet beyond what is visible.

Wet drywall, insulation and pad that cannot be dried are cut out and bagged. The remaining structure goes under air movers and dehumidifiers, a process explained on our structural drying page. Repair of the pipe itself is a plumber's job, and the finishing work follows once readings show the structure is dry.

Burst Pipe Claims and Your Insurer

Sudden pipe failures are commonly covered by homeowners policies, while long-term seepage often is not. Your policy language decides, so read the water damage section or ask your agent.

Report quickly, photograph everything and keep damaged materials until your insurer has seen them. The Illinois Department of Insurance says reasonable steps to prevent further damage are expected and that large permanent repairs should wait for the insurer's inspection.

Standards That Guide the Cleanup

The drying side of a burst pipe job follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. It calls for finding the source, classifying the water, setting a drying goal and documenting readings until the goal is met.

Pipe Failures in North Chicago Buildings

Lake County winters average well below freezing in January, with a mean near 15 degrees Fahrenheit, so pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls take a beating. In multi-unit buildings, a break in one unit often wets the unit below it, so landlords and tenants both need a fast plan.

The city's Building Department at 1850 Lewis Avenue handles permits and inspections, and the city posts a plumber registration application for contractors who work there. We cover North Chicago, and we also serve Winthrop Harbor and Fort Sheridan for the same kind of call.
