Where Kitchen Water Damage Starts

Kitchens combine plumbing, appliances and hard use, so leaks come from several directions. If water is active, shut the supply valve under the sink or at the main and call our 24/7 line.

Cabinets, Countertops and Flooring: Save or Replace?

Kitchen materials respond very differently to water, so decisions are made item by item. Solid wood and plywood cabinets often dry well. Particleboard swells and rarely recovers.

MaterialOften saved ifOften replaced if
Solid wood or plywood cabinetsDried quickly and not delaminatingWarped or mold-stained
Particleboard cabinetsRarelySwollen at the base or edges
Stone or laminate countertopsWater did not reach the substrateSubstrate swelling lifts the top
Tile floorSubfloor beneath driesSubfloor stays wet or tile lifts
Vinyl or laminate floorWater was brief and shallowEdges swell, seams open or water trapped beneath

How Kitchen Drying Works

Technicians pull toe kicks and, when needed, drill small holes to dry the cavities behind and under cabinets. A moisture meter and thermal camera show where water went, so only affected areas are opened.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and the crew rechecks readings. Floors may require special drying mats if the subfloor is wet. The process matches our structural drying work, and flooring questions lead to hardwood floor damage when relevant.

Insurance and Kitchen Leaks

Sudden kitchen leaks, such as a burst supply line or an overflowed dishwasher, are commonly covered for the resulting damage. Long-term seepage under a sink is often excluded as a maintenance issue.

Photograph under the sink and behind the dishwasher, save any parts that failed and keep receipts. We document the loss, and your insurer decides coverage.

Standard Reference for Kitchen Drying

Kitchen drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for water damage restoration. It guides how cabinet cavities, subfloors and walls are measured and dried before anything is rebuilt.

Safety Steps in a Wet Kitchen

Do these first

  • ✓Shut off the supply valve and unplug small appliances only if the floor is dry.
  • ✓Keep clear of the dishwasher and range if the area around them is wet.
  • ✓Do not use a gas range if water reached the gas line or controls.
