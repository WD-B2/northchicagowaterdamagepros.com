Emergency Storm Water Damage: Safe Steps During and After the Storm

We take storm calls 24/7, even in the middle of the weather. While you wait, stay away from downed power lines, avoid rooms with sagging ceilings and photograph the damage once it is safe to move around.

Do these first

  • ✓Never walk through standing water if power may be on, and call your utility about downed lines.
  • ✓Cover broken windows or roof openings with a tarp or plywood only if you can do it safely, and skip it during lightning or high wind.
  • ✓Move valuables away from wet ceilings and walls.
  • ✓Keep receipts for emergency repairs, since policies usually reimburse reasonable steps to prevent more damage.

How Storm Water Gets In

Roof damage

Missing shingles, lifted flashing and tree impact let rain through the roof deck and into insulation.

Windows and doors

Wind-driven rain pushes water past worn seals and into wall cavities.

Gutters and grading

Overflowing gutters and water sloping toward the foundation drive water into basements.

Sump and drain overload

Heavy rain can overwhelm sump pumps and floor drains, and see basement water damage for the next steps.

Where the water came from shapes the response. Rain through the roof is generally cleaner than ground-level flood water, which carries soil and contaminants.

How We Clean Up After a Storm

We extract standing water, tarp or board temporary openings where we can and map moisture across ceilings, walls and floors. Wet insulation is removed, since it holds water against framing for days.

Structures then dry under air movers and dehumidifiers with daily readings, as described in our structural drying section. If the ceiling is wet, see ceiling water damage. Permanent roof repair belongs to a roofer.

Storm Damage and Insurance

Wind and hail damage, and the rain that comes through the resulting openings, is commonly covered by homeowners policies. Flood from rising or surface water is generally excluded and requires separate flood insurance.

The Illinois Department of Insurance advises homeowners to report damage promptly, photograph it and keep damaged materials. Many storms produce many claims at once, so start your paperwork early and keep a log of calls with your insurer.

Reference Standard After a Storm

Storm cleanup relies on ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration, to sort water by category and to set drying goals. Rain that enters through a roof and ground-level flood water are handled differently under it.

Safety Steps After a Storm

Do these first

  • ✓Stay away from downed power lines and anything touching them.
  • ✓Do not enter a room with a sagging ceiling or standing water near outlets.
  • ✓Wait out lightning and high wind before checking the roof or tarping.
