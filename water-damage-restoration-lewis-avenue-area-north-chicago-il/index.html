When water gets into a home here, the first questions are where it came from and how long it has been sitting. Clean pipe water, rain that came in through a wall and sewer water need different handling, and we sort that out before we explain scope and price.

What the City Has Published About Drainage Here

The City of North Chicago describes chronic and severe flooding in the Southwest Pettibone Creek Watershed, caused by an aging and undersized storm sewer system. In its news release on the project, the city says homes in the area were built in the 1930s, 1940s and into the 1950s, when little attention was paid to how water flows.

Rain that falls east of Green Bay Road has long run downhill toward Lake Michigan, and the city says annual rainfall has increased since those homes were built. A project engineer quoted by the city said the work should take conditions from water reaching windows to a little water in the street.

The Lewis Avenue Detention Basin

The city is building a stormwater detention basin on a vacant parcel of about 3.5 acres near Lewis Avenue and 20th Street, just south of the middle and high schools. It is designed to hold about 20.5 acre-feet of water, and the city says state grant funding pays for it.

On July 27, 2026, about 3.5 inches of rain fell between 10 a.m. and 7 p.m. The city reported that the basin held about 5.7 acre-feet and released it over the next 18 hours, with no reports of flooding in residents’ yards upstream or downstream.

A second phase would replace undersized storm sewer segments, and the city expects both phases together to benefit about 742 residences and more than 900 structures. As of April 2026, bids for that second phase were still being discussed, so the full benefit has not yet been tested.

Why the Water Category Matters After a Storm

The industry standard, ANSI/IICRC S500, sorts water into three categories by how contaminated it is. Rain that has run across a street or a yard before it comes inside can be treated as contaminated, and sewer water is always in the most serious category.

The category decides what can be dried and what has to be removed. Carpet pad and insulation that sat in contaminated water are usually discarded, while clean-water leaks often leave framing and concrete that can be dried in place.

If Water Is Still Spreading

Use these steps while you wait, as long as every one of them is safe where you are.

Stop the source

If the water is pipe water, close the valve for that fixture or the main. If it is coming in from the yard or the street, do not wade out to stop it.

Watch the lower level

Do not go down into a flooded basement, because standing water can reach outlets, the panel and a furnace. Wait for the water to be assessed.

Photograph from a safe spot

Take pictures of the water line on walls and of stored items from the stairs or doorway. Note the time the water started coming in.

Call and describe

Call the 24/7 line and say whether the water looks clean, cloudy or sewage-like. That tells us what protection and equipment to plan for.
