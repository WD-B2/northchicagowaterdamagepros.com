Bubbled Paint From Water Damage in North Chicago, IL: Causes and Fixes

Bubbles in paint are a symptom, not the problem. This guide explains what is behind them, how to find the source and why repainting too soon makes the bubbles return.

Published by North Chicago Water Damage Pros · Updated October 2026
24/7 Emergency Dispatch(847) 368-7353☎ Call Now

Why Paint Bubbles When Water Is Involved

Paint forms a thin film that sticks to the surface beneath it. When moisture moves through the wall or ceiling and reaches the back of that film, the bond fails, and a pocket of water or vapor pushes the paint out into a bubble.

The pocket can be small and dry or large and full of water. Either way, it means moisture is present behind the paint, and a bubble that returns after you scrape it tells you the moisture never left.

If the pattern matches a ceiling, our ceiling water damage page goes deeper. If it matches a basement wall, see basement water damage.

Is It Water or Something Else?

Not every bubble is a leak. Painting over a dirty or glossy surface without priming can cause bubbling, as can applying oil paint over a damp latex coat or painting in high heat. The difference is that these bubbles are usually shallow and dry, and they do not return after proper repair.

Signs it is moisture

  • ✓The bubble feels damp, soft or cool, or releases water when pierced.
  • ✓There is a brown stain, a rust ring or a musty smell near the bubble.
  • ✓Drywall behind the paint is soft or crumbles when pressed.
  • ✓Bubbles keep returning in the same spot after repair.

What to Do Before You Touch the Paint

First, find and stop the source. A moisture meter on the wall or ceiling shows how far the damp area extends beyond the visible bubbles. A thermal camera can outline it without opening anything.

Do not pop the bubbles and repaint. That traps moisture and often leads to larger peeling and mold. If the area is wet, it needs to dry first, which our crews do with air movers and dehumidifiers following the logic of structural drying.

The Repair Sequence That Lasts

StepWhat happensWho usually does it
1. Stop the sourceRepair the pipe, roof or drainPlumber, roofer or HVAC technician
2. Measure and dryMoisture mapping, then drying equipmentRestoration crew
3. Remove failed materialsCut out soft drywall or insulationRestoration crew
4. Patch and primeRepair the surface and seal stainsDrywall contractor or painter
5. RepaintApply finish coats after readings are stablePainter

The order matters. Painting before step 2 is done wastes the labor, since moisture lifts the new paint the same way it lifted the old.

When Bubbles Mean Mold Risk

Prolonged moisture behind paint supports mold on the back of drywall paper. The Illinois Department of Public Health advises drying wet areas within 24 to 48 hours and having a professional clean mold areas larger than about 10 square feet.

If you see dark spots, fuzzy growth or smell musty, read our page on mold remediation before scrubbing. Disturbing growth without containment can spread it.

What a Small Bubble Might Cost You

Cost depends on the source and the size of the wet area. A single bubble from a one-time splash may need only a patch and paint. A ceiling stain from a leaking pipe can involve drywall removal, insulation, drying and repair.

Scope and price come after we measure the moisture, and we explain them before work starts. The most expensive path is usually repainting three times before someone finds the leak.

Humidity and Condensation Bubbles

Not all bubbling comes from a leak. Steam from showers and cooking can condense on cool walls and ceilings, and bathrooms without a working exhaust fan are the usual victims. The paint softens, and bubbles form near the shower or on the ceiling above it.

If the bubbles are limited to a humid room and a moisture meter reads normal behind them, the fix is ventilation, not plumbing. Run the exhaust fan during and after showers, and use a washable bathroom paint when you refinish.

Tools That Help Find the Source

ToolWhat it showsLimits
Pin moisture meterMoisture in drywall and wood at a pointLeaves small holes and reads only where you probe
Pinless moisture meterMoisture below the surface across an areaCan be thrown off by metal behind the wall
Thermal cameraCool, damp areas as color patternsShows temperature differences and needs interpretation
Flashlight at a low angleRaised bubbles and texture changesShows the surface only

A technician combines these tools and the building layout to trace water back to its entry point. That is usually faster and less destructive than cutting several test holes in a ceiling.

Standards Behind the Moisture Checks

Technicians use the principles in ANSI/IICRC S500 when they measure a wet wall, which stresses recording readings before, during and after drying. A painter has no such standard to rely on, which is why drying should come before paint.

Paint, Humidity and Lake County Seasons

Lake County has humid summers and cold winters, a January mean near 15 degrees and a July mean near 82. Winter condensation on cold exterior walls and summer humidity in basements can both stress paint.
