What to Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage in North Chicago

After water damage, the question is not just what is wet. It is what the water was, how long it stayed and how porous each item is. This guide sorts it out.

Published by North Chicago Water Damage Pros · Updated November 2026
24/7 Emergency Dispatch(847) 368-7353☎ Call Now

Two Questions Decide Almost Everything

First, what kind of water was it? Second, is the item porous? Clean water from a pipe leaves more items salvageable than flood water or sewage, and hard non-porous items survive far better than soft, absorbent ones.

The industry standard ANSI/IICRC S500 describes three water categories. Category 1 is clean supply water, Category 2 carries some contamination and Category 3 is grossly contaminated, such as sewage or rising flood water.

Food, Medicine and Things You Put in Your Body

Food and drinks that touched flood water or sewage should be thrown out, including canned goods with damaged seals and anything with a cardboard or porous package. Food stored in a refrigerator that lost power for a long time may also be unsafe.

Medicines and cosmetics that were soaked or contaminated are not worth the risk. If you are unsure about a prescription, ask your pharmacist. When in doubt, throw it out, especially with infants, older adults or anyone with a weak immune system in the home.

Furniture, Bedding and Soft Goods

Upholstered furniture soaks up water and holds it deep inside foam and padding. If it got wet with clean water and was dried quickly, a professional may be able to save it. If it sat for days or took contaminated water, it usually has to go.

Mattresses are especially hard to dry. Even with clean water, the interior stays damp and supports mold. Bedding and clothing can often be laundered if the water was clean, and a hot wash helps. For contaminated water, discard unless the item is irreplaceable and a specialist can clean it.

Document Before You Discard

Photograph each item before it leaves the house. Note the brand, age and an approximate value, and keep a few samples if your adjuster asks. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises keeping damaged materials, or documentation of them, until the insurer has inspected.

A simple routine

  • ✓Take photos of items where they sit, then again after you pull them out.
  • ✓Write a list with brand, age, price and condition.
  • ✓Keep receipts for replacements and emergency steps.
  • ✓Ask your adjuster how they want contaminated items handled before disposal.

Handling Contaminated Items Safely

Wear gloves and boots when handling sewage or flood-contaminated items, bag them in heavy plastic and wash your hands well afterward. Do not bring contaminated items into clean areas of the home.

Our crews handle removal and disposal under protective procedures, which matters most on large losses. See sewage backup cleanup for how that works.

Appliances, Toys and Household Goods

Small appliances that took on water should not be plugged in until a technician checks them, since moisture inside can cause shocks or fires. Larger appliances, such as refrigerators and washers, may be cleanable on the outside but need a service visit before use.

Children's toys that are porous, such as stuffed animals, are usually discarded after contaminated water. Hard plastic toys can be washed and sanitized. Cutting boards, dishes and pots that were in contaminated water should be washed in hot water and sanitized if they are non-porous, or replaced if they are wood or plastic with cracks.

Sentimental Items and Heirlooms

Some items are worth more than their materials. Quilts, wedding dresses and family heirlooms can sometimes be cleaned by a specialist, especially after clean water. Do not scrub them. Rinse gently if mud is present, lay them flat to dry out of the sun and ask a conservator or specialty cleaner about options.

Make a list of these items before the crew arrives, so they can be moved safely and photographed. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises keeping documentation of damaged property for your insurer.

Deciding Together as a Household

Sorting after water damage is emotional, and different family members value different things. Set aside a small box for items that need a second look, and agree on a date to make final decisions so the area can be dried and rebuilt.

Sorting After Lake County Floods and Leaks

Lake County has seen flood events in 1996, 2000, 2004, 2013 and 2017, according to its hazard plan, and basements are where sorting questions usually arise. Stored boxes, furniture and old carpet in a basement are the items families struggle to decide on.

We serve North Chicago and also respond in Winthrop Harbor and Fort Sheridan. Photos and a short list before the crew arrives make the sorting conversation easier.
