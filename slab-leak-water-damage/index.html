Emergency Slab Leak Response: What To Do While You Wait

Slab leak calls get answered 24/7 because a leak under a slab can wet flooring and walls quickly. While you wait, shut off the main water supply, keep electronics and rugs off the wet floor and call a plumber to locate the break.

First steps

  • ✓Close the main water valve and see if the sound of running water stops.
  • ✓Lift rugs, mats and boxes off damp flooring and keep cords out of wet areas.
  • ✓Check the water meter. If it keeps moving with every fixture off, the leak is active.
  • ✓Write down where floors feel warm, damp or musty so the plumber and the crew can pinpoint the area.

Why Is My Floor Warm or My Water Bill Climbing?

Most slab leaks never announce themselves with a puddle. The symptoms below are the ones homeowners usually report, and any one of them is a reason to check.

Warm spots on the floor

A leak in a hot water line can warm the concrete above it.

A higher bill with no change in use

Leaking water is still metered, so consumption rises without anyone noticing a fixture running.

Sound of running water

A hiss or trickle with everything off can mean a pressurized line is leaking beneath the slab.

Damp carpet, buckling floors or musty smell

Water rising through a slab wets whatever sits on top of it.

If these signs match your home, the first call is to a plumber who can test and locate the leak. The water the leak released is what we handle, and the crew works around the plumber's access needs.

How a Slab Leak Cleanup Works

The crew maps moisture across floors and lower walls with meters and thermal imaging to find how far water has spread. Flooring that cannot be dried is removed, which is often carpet and pad, laminate and sometimes hardwood.

Concrete itself holds moisture, so drying a slab takes longer than drying wood. Dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air and the slab surface over several days, following the logic of our structural drying work.

Choosing Between Repair Paths for the Pipe

Plumbing approachWhat it means for the cleanup
Break the slab and replace the sectionDust and debris, and new wet areas to dry
Reroute the line through walls or atticLess slab work, but wall openings to dry and repair
Pipe lining or repair inside the lineSmaller disruption, so cleanup is mostly about the existing damage

Your plumber decides which path fits the pipe, and the decision is theirs. We simply adjust drying around whichever route the plumber chooses.

Slab Leaks and Insurance

Policies differ a lot on slab leaks. Some cover the resulting water damage but not the cost of reaching the pipe, and some exclude long-term leakage. Ask your agent which parts of your policy apply before authorizing demolition.

Keep the plumber's written leak-detection report, since adjusters usually ask for it. We document moisture readings and affected areas in writing, and your insurer decides coverage.

Standards for Drying a Slab and What Sits on It

Drying after a slab leak follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard reference for professional water damage restoration. Concrete is slow to release moisture, so the standard's emphasis on measuring and documenting readings matters even more here.
