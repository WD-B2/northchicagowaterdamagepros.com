Water Damage Insurance Claims in North Chicago, IL: Step by Step

A water damage claim goes better when the paperwork is built as the cleanup happens. These steps follow the Illinois Department of Insurance guidance and what we see in the field.

Published by North Chicago Water Damage Pros · Updated October 2026
24/7 Emergency Dispatch(847) 368-7353☎ Call Now

Step 1: Stop the Damage and Stay Safe

Before anything else, stop the water if you can and keep people away from electrical hazards. Insurers expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and the Illinois Department of Insurance says those emergency steps are allowed even before the adjuster visits.

Reasonable means things like shutting off the water, extracting standing water, moving belongings and covering a roof opening. It does not mean large permanent repairs, which the department advises waiting on until the insurer has inspected.

Step 2: Report the Loss Promptly

Call your insurer or agent as soon as you can. Most policies require prompt notice, and a late report gives the insurer a reason to question the damage. Write down the claim number, the date and the name of each person you speak to.

You do not need to understand the full extent of the damage before you call. A short description of what happened is enough to open the claim, and details can follow.

Step 3: Document Before You Clean

Photographs and video do most of the work in a claim. Shoot wide shots of each room and close-ups of damaged items, the source of the water and any visible waterlines.

What to recordWhy it helps
Photos of every wet room before cleanupShows the original condition and scale
The source of the waterHelps decide whether the cause is covered
Damaged items with brand, age and approximate valueSupports the contents claim
Dates and times of discovery and each callBuilds a clean timeline
Moisture readings and equipment logsShow what was wet and when it dried
Receipts for emergency stepsSupport reimbursement of reasonable protective costs

Our crews photograph the work and record moisture readings as part of the job. See our structural drying page for how those readings are kept.

Step 4: Keep Damaged Materials Until the Insurer Says Otherwise

It is tempting to throw everything out. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises keeping damaged materials, or at least samples and photographs, until the insurer has had a chance to inspect.

That does not mean living with a wet carpet. Photograph it, cut out and keep a sample, and let the crew remove the rest. If an item is contaminated, such as sewage-soaked material, ask your adjuster how they want it documented before disposal.

Step 5: Meet the Adjuster and Share Your Records

The adjuster inspects the damage, reviews your documentation and writes an estimate. You can share your photos, our written scope and the moisture log. If our scope and the adjuster's estimate differ, ask for the differences in writing.

Your insurer decides coverage, and we do not tell you what will be approved. We explain what we found and why each step is needed, which usually shortens the conversation.

What Happens If the Loss Is Not Covered

If your insurer denies the claim or covers only part of it, ask for the reason in writing and the policy language it relied on. Flood and long-term seepage are frequent exclusions, and sewer backup depends on added coverage.

You can still dry and repair the property. We explain scope and price before work starts, and for a loss that is probably not covered, that conversation matters more, since the cost is yours.

Mold, Contents and Living Expenses

A covered water loss may bring related coverage questions. Mold after a covered event may be covered or capped, personal property has its own limit, and some policies include temporary living expenses when a home cannot be lived in. Read those sections of the policy or ask your agent. Our page on mold remediation explains how mold follows moisture.

Working With the Adjuster's Estimate

When the adjuster sends an estimate, read it line by line against your photos and the written scope. Look for rooms that were left out, materials listed as repair when they were removed and equipment days that do not match the moisture log.

If something is missing, send the evidence that supports it, such as a photo, a reading or a receipt, and ask for a written reply. Keep the tone factual. Disputes usually resolve faster when each point is tied to a document.

Keep a Simple Claim Log

A one-page log keeps the file organized. Record each call with the date, the person and what was said, and add the claim number to every email and invoice.

Log entryExample
Date and time of lossThe day you found the water and when it started, if known

Standards Behind the Documentation

A claim file is stronger when the work follows a recognized standard. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference for water damage restoration, and its emphasis on classification and moisture records is what we use to build the file your adjuster reviews.

Claims After Lake County Storms and Freezes

Lake County winters average well below freezing, and July storms can be violent, with the county plan recording the July 2011 derecho. After a freeze or a storm, adjusters get many claims at once, so organized documentation helps your file stay moving.
