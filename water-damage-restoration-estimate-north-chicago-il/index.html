Water Damage Restoration Estimate in North Chicago: What You Pay For

A water damage estimate should read like a map of the job. This guide explains the common line items, why exact prices cannot be quoted blind and how to compare estimates fairly.

Published by North Chicago Water Damage Pros · Updated November 2026
24/7 Emergency Dispatch(847) 368-7353☎ Call Now

Why No One Can Quote a Price Without Seeing the Loss

Water behaves unpredictably. It moves under floors, wicks up walls and hides in cavities, so the visible damage is rarely the whole story. A fair estimate comes after a technician measures what is wet.

That is why we do not list exact prices on this site. We explain scope and price before work starts, and the factors below show what moves the number. Be cautious of any company that names a firm total over the phone for a loss it has not seen.

The Parts of a Typical Estimate

Line itemWhat it coversWhat drives it
Emergency response and assessmentTechnician visit, moisture mapping, source checkSize of the property and complexity
Water extractionPumps, wet vacs, weighted extractorsDepth and area of water
Demolition and removalCutting out wet drywall, insulation, padWhich materials cannot be dried
Drying equipmentAir movers, LGR dehumidifiers, drying matsNumber of units and days on site
MonitoringDaily moisture readings and logsDays of drying
Cleaning and antimicrobial stepsSanitizing surfaces and contentsWater category
Containment and air filtrationPlastic barriers and HEPA machinesContamination or mold
DisposalBagging and hauling debrisVolume of removed material

Rebuild is often a separate estimate from a contractor, which is why comparing a mitigation estimate with a full project estimate is misleading. Our page on wall and drywall repair explains where the cleanup ends and the repair begins.

Equipment Days Explained

Drying equipment is usually billed per unit per day. A small wet room may need a few air movers and one dehumidifier for a few days. A flooded basement may need many units for longer.

Good estimates tie equipment to the readings. The industry reference, ANSI/IICRC S500, emphasizes measurement, and a technician should be able to explain why each unit is placed where it is. If the number of units seems high, ask for the reasoning.

Water Category Changes the Price

CategorySourceWhat it adds
Category 1Clean supply waterBaseline extraction and drying
Category 2Some contamination, such as washing machine dischargeMore removal and cleaning
Category 3Sewage, flood or groundwaterProtective gear, sanitizing, disposal, more removal

Because category affects what must be removed, it can change an estimate more than size does. See our pages on sewage backup cleanup and flood damage cleanup for how contaminated losses unfold.

Red Flags in an Estimate

Things worth questioning

  • ✓A firm price before anyone has seen or measured the loss.
  • ✓No mention of moisture readings or monitoring.
  • ✓A single lump sum with no line items.
  • ✓Pressure to sign before you have read the scope.
  • ✓Promises that a claim will be approved or that water will never return.

How to Compare Two Estimates Fairly

QuestionWhy it matters
What stage does each estimate cover?Mitigation and full project numbers are not comparable
How many equipment units and days?Equipment is a large line item
Which materials are removed and which are dried?Removal changes both cost and risk
What water category does each assume?The category drives cleaning and disposal
What documentation is included?You need records for your insurer

If two estimates still differ widely, ask each company to walk you through its scope. The difference usually becomes clear when you hear the reasoning.

Estimates and Insurance

Your insurer may write its own estimate, and it can differ from a restoration company's scope. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises photographing damage and keeping receipts, and a written scope and moisture log give you something concrete to discuss with your adjuster.

We document the loss in writing. Coverage is your insurer's decision, and we do not promise that a claim will be approved.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

A conversation before signing saves money and frustration. Ask the technician to point to each line item on the loss itself, and ask what would change the number.

Questions that reveal quality

  • ✓What would make this estimate go up or down once work starts?
  • ✓How will you tell me when the scope changes, and will I approve it first?
  • ✓How many drying units are you placing, and where?
  • ✓When will equipment be removed, and what readings tell you it is dry?
  • ✓What records will I receive at the end?

Understanding Deductibles and Your Share

Insurance may pay for part of a covered loss after your deductible. The deductible is the amount you pay before coverage applies, and it varies by policy, so check yours before work starts.

If the estimate is close to or below your deductible, you may decide to handle the work without filing. That is a personal choice, and an agent can explain how claims may affect your policy renewal.

When to Get a Second Opinion

A second opinion makes sense when the scope seems very different from what you see, when the price is far above or below others or when the company will not explain a line item. Ask a second company to look at the same loss and compare scope, not just totals.

Estimates in Lake County Winters and Spring Thaws

Cold weather slows drying, which can extend equipment days. Lake County's January mean sits near 15 degrees, and a freeze followed by a thaw can add several rooms to a loss in a short time.
