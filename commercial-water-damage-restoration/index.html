Emergency Commercial Water Damage: Secure the Building While You Wait

Commercial dispatch runs 24/7, including after-hours alarms and weekend discoveries. While you wait, stop the water at the nearest shutoff, clear people from affected areas and move critical records and inventory above the waterline.

Business priorities

  • ✓Shut off water to the affected zone, or the building main if you cannot isolate it.
  • ✓Turn off power to wet areas only through the panel and only if it is dry. Call the electrician or building engineer if not.
  • ✓Move inventory, servers and paperwork to dry space and photograph everything before it moves.
  • ✓Notify your landlord or property manager and your insurance carrier early, and log who you spoke to and when.

How a Commercial Cleanup Differs From a Home Cleanup

Commercial spaces have larger floor plates, shared walls and systems that serve other tenants. A technician has to isolate the affected area, protect neighbors and keep egress and fire safety clear while equipment runs.

Drying plans account for materials such as suspended ceilings, commercial carpet tile, slab floors and metal studs. The scale and equipment follow the same logic as our water extraction and structural drying work, with more attention to scheduling around staff and customers.

Documentation That Helps a Business Claim

Business insurance can involve property coverage, contents, and sometimes business income coverage. What your policy includes depends on its terms, and your insurer decides.

Good records move a claim faster. We record affected areas, moisture readings, photographs and equipment days, and give you a written scope so your adjuster can review it. Keep invoices for emergency steps and note any interruption to operations.

Questions Property Managers Ask

Can part of the space stay open?

Sometimes. Containment and equipment placement can isolate wet areas, depending on the layout and water category.

Who coordinates with other trades?

We coordinate with plumbers, electricians and roofers so drying and repair do not conflict.

What about neighboring tenants?

Technicians check adjoining spaces for moisture and protect common areas during the work.

How is progress reported?

Moisture logs and equipment records are updated at each visit and can be shared with the property manager.

For losses that start with weather, see storm water damage. For losses that start with a failed pipe, see burst pipe cleanup.

Standards Used on Commercial Losses

Commercial drying plans reference ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration, along with the building owner's access and safety rules. The standard's emphasis on documentation is what makes records useful to property managers and insurers.

Safety Steps for Staff and Tenants

Do these first

  • ✓Move people out of wet areas and keep exits clear of cords and equipment.
  • ✓Do not enter areas with standing water near panels, server racks or elevators.
  • ✓Post a notice and keep tenants and customers out of the affected zone until it is assessed.
